2026-11-21 · UI directions · One UI · sheet 02

Search keeps the shape of a root screen: a bare large title in the upper third, then the field and the settings gear as the row that closes it. Under an empty field, every folder and the eight biggest albums are small neutral cards that carry their own artwork, where today they are tiles in eight fixed colours.

Where the field sits: under the title at rest and while results are being read, and on the keyboard while it is open, because that is where the thumb already is. How results are sorted: a collection is an object and a song is a row, so matching artists, folders and albums come first as a shelf and matching songs follow in one card.

What was kept

  • Results on every keystroke. No submit step, no history, no suggestions.
  • The matching rule, including its limits: up to 5 artists and 10 albums, folders searched, user playlists not searched.
  • The type words "Artist", "Playlist" and "Album, {artist}", now as the second line under a shelf tile.
  • "Clear search" at the end of the field once there is text.
  • Tap a song to play every matching song from that row, context "Search results". Long-press or the more button opens the track menu.
  • Browse holds every folder, then the eight albums with the most songs. Tap opens it.
  • The no-results strings, word for word.

Where the sheet departs from the brief

The brief asks frame B to mix an artist, albums, a playlist and songs for "dhur". Under the app's matching rule that query returns two albums and ten songs and nothing else: artists and folders are matched on their own names only. Frame B shows what the app would return, the same result set as the baseline sheet, and frame F uses "ph", which returns all four types.

Open questions

  • The field moves. It is under the title until the keyboard opens, then on the keyboard. The gain is reach while typing; the cost is that it travels about 380dp each time the keyboard opens or closes. The alternative is to leave it pinned under the bar in every state, as frame B has it.
  • Collections before songs. Today's order is artists, folders, songs, albums. The shelf puts albums ahead of songs, which fixes the case in frame B and changes the order the owner is used to. A query with more than three matching collections scrolls the shelf sideways.
  • Browse tiles without colour. The kit lets a tile take a trace of its cover's colour (tinted wash). The sheet leaves them neutral, because the direction spends cover colour only on progress and state. Twelve tinted tiles would be livelier and would break that rule.
  • User playlists. "Late drive" and "Rahman, slow" cannot be found by search today, and still cannot here. If that is an oversight in the app, they would join the shelf as "Playlist".
