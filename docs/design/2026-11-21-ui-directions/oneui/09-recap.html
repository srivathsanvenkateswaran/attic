Recap

2026-11-21 · UI directions · One UI · sheet 09

Recap does everything it does today: four periods, six stats, top songs and artists, languages, the listening clock, and a ten-card story whose cards save and share as 9:16 images. What changes is where the look comes from. The page is an ordinary screen from the kit: a large title, the periods as sub-tabs, grouped cards. The cards stop being posters in a fixed five-colour palette and become pieces of the app: a dark stepped ground, one grouped card, superellipse art, and a fill that carries the number.

The sheet settles the card family (C), the story's chrome (D), where progress moves from thin segments across the top to one thick bar in a deck under the card, and how the language shares are coloured.

What was kept

  • The four periods, one always selected, "This year" by default, and all period wording, including the switch on 24 December.
  • The six stats and their labels. "Share a card" with all ten thumbnails; tapping one opens the story at that card.
  • "Top songs" and "Top artists" to ten rows, with plays and minutes. Tap plays from that song, long-press opens the track menu, tap on an artist opens the artist. "Play" starts the ten from the top, or pauses when that list is playing.
  • "Languages", "When you listen" with the clock, the listener type and the busiest hour, and the log footnote.
  • The story: ten cards in the same order, skipped when their data is missing, 7 seconds each, tap the left third to go back, tap elsewhere to go on, hold to pause, the last card stays.
  • Every sentence on every card. "Save" to Pictures/Attic with its two messages, "Share" as a 1080 × 1920 PNG of the card alone.
  • The mark and the period label on every card. The loading and empty-period states. The mini player and tabs on the page; neither in the story.

Why the languages are not in cover colours

The first draft gave each language the colour of its most-played cover, which would have matched the rest of this direction. The kit sets every cover colour to the same lightness, so Hindi's orange and Tamil's green sit side by side at equal brightness, and a palette check puts that pair at ΔE 1.5 for red-green colour-blind readers: indistinguishable. Lightness steps of one hue do not have that problem and need no per-language table.

Kit additions in this sheet

All in the sheet's <style>, ready to fold into kit.css: the share-card canvas .rc with its scaler .rcfit, the listening clock, .langbar and the accent steps .st0 to .st3, the story deck, the play row, and .label.has-btn for a label with a button on its line.

Open questions

  • Listener type and the December switch. Both are on the list of Spotify cues in CURRENT-UI.md section 5, and both are kept here because the brief keeps every string outside the naming table. Dropping the type leaves the clock card with the busiest-hour sentence; dropping the switch leaves "Your 2026 so far" until the year ends.
  • Cards are always dark. Today the card colours ignore the theme, and these do too. A light set from the light tokens is possible if a shared image should match the phone it came from.
  • The stamp on cover-washed cards. On the top song, top artist and summary cards the mark's box takes the cover's colour, because the direction keeps amber out of tinted surfaces. If the stamp should be Lamplight on every image that leaves the app, that is one exception to that rule.
  • "4 of 10". The count beside the progress bar is new text. Without it the bar still shows position, but not how many cards there are.
  • More than four languages. The app knows six languages plus "No vocals" and "other". The accent has four readable steps on a dark card. A fifth and sixth language would share the darkest step and rely on their rows.
  • "412 songs played for the first time". In the log's first year this highlight equals the "Songs" stat. That is today's behaviour, drawn as it would appear; the item could be skipped when the log began inside the period.
