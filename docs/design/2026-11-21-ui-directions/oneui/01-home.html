Home

2026-11-21 · UI directions · One UI · sheet 01

Home is rebuilt on One UI's split between a viewing area and an interaction area. At rest the top third holds only the title and a count, and the first thing under the thumb is a tray of six shortcuts. Scrolling collapses the title into a compact bar and the page continues as grouped cards.

One rule decides how every section is drawn: a collection is an object, a song is a row. Albums, folders, playlists and artists are superellipse art with a name underneath, in trays and shelves. Songs are rows inside a card, split by inset hairlines. The sheet settles that rule, the sub-tabs that replace the filter chips, and the dock that joins the mini player to the tab bar.

What was kept

  • Every section and its order: shortcuts, Recap entry, Instant mixes, "Recently played", "Start listening", "Your folders", "Albums", "More from", "Top artists".
  • The four filters, exactly one selected, "All" by default. Each other filter leaves only its own section.
  • Favourites always first among the shortcuts, up to six in total.
  • Tap on an Instant mix starts radio, long-press opens the track menu.
  • Settings is one tap from Home. The mini player keeps cover, title, artist, heart, play or pause, swipe and tap.
  • The empty-library message, word for word.

Open questions

  • The count under the title. "127 songs on this phone" is lifted from the About paragraph in Settings. It is the only string on Home that is not there today. The alternative is a title with nothing under it.
  • The mark above the title. It is on Home only. Library and Search show a bare title. If that reads as a logo screen, it goes and the title moves up 22dp.
  • Instant mixes as rows. Rows show the artist, which the cover grid never did, but the nine songs now take two swipes to see in full. A 3 × 3 tray like the shortcuts is the fallback; it would put two trays one above the other.
  • Settings as a gear. One UI would more often put Settings behind a "more options" menu. A direct gear keeps today's one-tap reach.
