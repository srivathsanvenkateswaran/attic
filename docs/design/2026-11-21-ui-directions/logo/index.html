Logo concepts

2026-11-21 · UI directions · logo

Asked for alongside the UI round: the current mark "is really bad". It reads as the generic home button, says nothing about music, and its green is Spotify's. Three designers each took one territory and drew four concepts, twelve in all. Every mark is a single-colour path on Android's 108dp adaptive-icon canvas, kept inside the 66dp safe circle, so each one already works as a launcher icon, a themed icon and a 24dp notification icon.

Each card shows the colour launcher icon, the mark in one colour on light and dark, the themed (wallpaper-tinted) icon, and the mark at 24dp. The colourways are the designers' own and are independent of the UI direction; the mark can be recoloured to whichever direction is chosen.

Today's mark, for comparison: a house with a round window in Spotify green.

Shortlist, in place

Three I would take forward, one from each territory, shown where the mark actually lives: on a home screen in colour and themed, in the app bar, in the status bar, and beside the name.

The letter A

A monogram. The capital A is already a roof: two rafters and a tie beam. The risk in this territory is a generic A that any app could wear.

lettershortlist

Side a

A lowercase a whose bowl is a record with a spindle hole, the stem and hook sitting over it like a tonearm. It is the initial and a record at once, and the name is what is printed on a record's first side.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for an @ sign at a glance. Audacy, a radio app, also uses a lowercase a: put the two side by side before committing.

letter

Hold

A capital A with its crossbar dropped and a dot under the ridge. That shape is the short fermata, the notation sign for "hold". The most robust of the set at small sizes.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for a pendant lamp or a tent. An A with a dot could belong to several brands; avoid a blue version, which drifts toward Arch Linux and Android Auto.

letter

Loft

A heavy A cut by one groove, like a record. The piece the groove leaves whole is the triangle under the ridge, which is the attic. Best at 48px and above; the groove thins out at 24.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for an A-frame cabin, so an outdoor brand. AIMP, a music player, uses a triangular A.

letter

Tie beam

An A whose crossbar is a seek bar running past both rafters: thick where played, a round thumb, thin for the rest. The only one that says "player" outright, but it needs two colours to do it.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for a struck-through A or the austral sign. In one colour the played and remaining halves merge.

The architecture of an attic

The roof space itself: rafters, gables, the section through the top of a house. The risk is drawing another home button.

archshortlist

Rafters

Five upright bars with their tops cut on a 45° pitch. It is a gable seen as rafters and a level meter at the same moment, and it loses nothing at 24px.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for a bar chart, so an analytics or fitness app. The nearest well-known shape is the Adidas mountain (three slanted bars); Deezer's old mark was stacked columns.

arch

Roof beam

Two beamed notes whose beam is bent into a gable. The fastest of all twelve to understand.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for Apple Music, whose icon is a beamed pair of notes; Samsung Music also uses a note. It is also the closest of this row to a house.

arch

Eave

A play triangle under a lopsided roof whose long slope runs parallel to the triangle's top edge, all on 30° geometry. Says "player" and holds at every size.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for a paper-plane send icon, or a home-cinema logo. Most of its area is a play triangle, which nobody can own.

arch

Eject

The attic in section: a gable with a round window over a floor line, which is also the eject key on a tape deck. The strongest silhouette of the row.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for the eject key, since it is one, which on a player can read as "remove". Shares its idea with Deck below; only one of the two should go forward.

Records and sound

Music as a thing you own and keep under the roof. The risk is the clichés: a bare disc, a note, a play triangle.

sound

Deck

The same eject key as a 45° roof over a floor line, with half a record standing under the ridge. Roof and record both read without the name.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for "eject" or "remove the SD card". Worth showing to a few people cold before choosing it.

soundshortlist

Headroom

A speaker cabinet with its top cut at 30° to fit under the roof slope, the tweeter tucked under the high side. The steadiest of this row at 24px.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for a generic speaker icon, or a camera at very small sizes. The attic is more in the name than in the drawing.

sound

Lid

A box whose roof is hinged at one eave and lifted like the lid of a music box, propped open on a half record. It is today's house with the roof opened, so it keeps a thread to the current mark.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for an open box, so storage or shipping. Three pieces, and it goes soft at 24px.

sound

Corner

A record sliding out of a sleeve that stands on its corner, so the corner is the ridge. Clean in colour.

Top: launcher icon in colour, then one colour on light and dark. Bottom: launcher at 48 and 36dp, themed light and dark, and the 24dp notification icon.

Could be mistaken for an eraser or a tag. Nobody sees a roof unless told.

Wordmark

From the letter set: the Side a mark with "attic" set in Figtree SemiBold, converted to paths. Figtree is the app's current typeface; the wordmark should be reset in the chosen direction's typeface.

The call

Side a is the one I would ship. It is the only concept of the twelve that needs no roof to be Attic's: the initial and the record are the same drawing, it survives at 24dp in one colour, and it gives a wordmark for free (the mark can stand as the first letter of the name). It also moves furthest from the current house.

Rafters is the alternative if you want the attic in the picture. It is the simplest shape here and the most robust small, at the cost of looking a little like a chart.

Headroom is the safe, literal option: a speaker that fits under a roof. It will never be misread, and it is the least distinctive of the three.

What to check before committing

  • None of the "could be mistaken for" notes come from a trademark or image search. They are the designers' recall of other marks. Side a against Audacy's lowercase a is the first comparison to make.
  • Eject and Deck are the same idea from two territories. If the eject key appeals, choose between them rather than keeping both.
  • Two marks (Headroom, Corner) cut their holes with the even-odd fill rule and need android:fillType="evenOdd" in the VectorDrawable. Roof beam relies on the non-zero rule.
  • The colourways are placeholders for a decision that depends on the UI direction. On Pixel the launcher icon is mostly seen themed, in the wallpaper's colours, where only the silhouette matters.
