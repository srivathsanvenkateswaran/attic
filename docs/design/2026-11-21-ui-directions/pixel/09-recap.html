09 · Recap

2026-11-21 · UI directions · Pixel · sheet 09

Recap keeps everything Wrapped does today: four periods, six stats, top songs and artists, languages, the listening clock, and ten 9:16 cards that play in sequence and can be saved or shared. What changes is the form. The page is built from the same containers as Home. The cards are drawn from the expressive shape set and the width and weight axes of Google Sans Flex, in a scheme taken from one of the period's covers. The sequence plays on a screen laid out like Now Playing, with one wavy line for progress in place of a row of segments.

The page follows the wallpaper scheme. A card never does: its roles are fixed from a cover, so the image someone receives is the one the sender saw.

How a card gets its colour

A card about one song or one artist takes that cover. The other cards are dealt from the covers of the period's top songs so that neighbours differ. From the cover only the hue is used; tone and chroma are fixed per role, the same recipe as .from-art, so a grey cover and a loud one give cards of equal contrast. The roles are the dark art scheme in both themes. The grounds are surfaceContainer (cards 1, 6, 7, 8, 10), primaryContainer (3, 5) and the pale primary or tertiaryContainer (2, 4, 9). A song with no cover uses the app's fallback seed.

The story screen takes the scheme of the card it is showing, the way Now Playing takes the cover's, and that scheme changes on the slow effects spring when the card does.

What was kept

  • The four periods with one always selected and “This year” as the default; every headline, intro line and period label in the period table, including the 24 December switch.
  • The six stats and their labels; “Share a card” with 118dp thumbnails that open the story at that card.
  • “Top songs”, up to 10, with play counts and a play control that plays the list or toggles pause; tap plays from that song with the context “Your top songs”; long-press opens the track menu. “Top artists”, up to 10, with minutes; tap opens the artist; no two artists share a cover.
  • “Languages” whenever a listened song has a known language; “When you listen” with the 24-hour clock, the listener type, its line and the busiest hour; the footnote with the date the log began.
  • The ten card types, their order, the rule that a card with no data is skipped, and every string on them. The mark and the period label on every card.
  • The fixed 360 × 640 canvas, scaled for the story, the thumbnails and the export. The export is a 1080 × 1920 PNG of the card alone, with square corners and none of the screen around it.
  • “Save” to Pictures/Attic with both snackbar messages; “Share” through the system share sheet.
  • Seven seconds a card; the last card stays; tap the left third for the previous card and anywhere else for the next, or to close on the last; press and hold to pause; the timer also stops during an export and behind the share sheet; back closes. The minute count still counts up over 1.6 s.
  • The four listener types. Each now has a glyph at the clock's hub: bedtime, wb_twilight, wb_sunny, nights_stay.
  • The loading and empty-period states. The mini player and navigation bar stay on the page; the story covers them.

Motion

  • The line. The seek bar's wave (32dp wavelength, 4dp stroke), its phase moving while the timer runs. On hold the amplitude springs to zero and the card scales to 0.93 with its corners going from 28 to 40, both on the default spatial spring. The thumb passes a stop at each card boundary.
  • Changing card. The pager slides one card on the default spatial spring and the sliver widens into the card. The screen's scheme cross-fades on slow effects.
  • On a card. The minute count counts up, as today. Shapes arrive by scaling from 0.8 on the fast spatial spring, staggered 40 ms. Nothing loops, so a saved frame is the settled drawing.

Open questions

  • Colour from covers, or one palette for every user. From covers, no colour on a shared image belongs to Attic, and two people's cards look different. A period whose top covers share a hue gives ten cards in that hue. The alternative is a fixed seed for all cards, which makes them recognisable as Attic's and less personal.
  • Languages have no colour of their own. Today Tamil is always marigold and Hindi always pink. Here the tone marks rank within the period, and the name carries the language. If a language should look the same from one period to the next, it needs a fixed hue per language, which is the one place this direction would hold colours of its own.
  • One line instead of ten segments. Segments show the count at a glance; the line shows it as stops and the bar says “5 of 10”. If the stops read too faintly on a phone, they can become gaps in the track.
  • Swiping. The sliver suggests a sideways swipe. The proposal is that a swipe does what the taps do. Today there are only taps.
  • The summary names one song and one artist. Someone sharing it may want all ten names on the image. The fallback is two five-row grouped lists like card 4, which brings back more of today's layout.
  • Card strings were kept because the brief's table does not list them. “The artists on repeat” is close to the name of a Spotify playlist; “The artists you played most” would be a neutral line.
  • The file name is attic-wrapped-{card}-{timestamp}.png today. It should become attic-recap-… with the rename.
  • The line on the Minutes card stops where the date falls in the period (89% of 2026). Nobody is told that. It can be a complete line with no loss.
  • Six stat shapes. Only the cookie carries meaning; the rest are variety. The quieter fallback is the kit's two-column .stats grid with Minutes on the accent tone.
