11 · Equaliser

2026-11-25 · UI directions · Pixel · sheet 11

Superseded. This mock shows the 1.1.0 look. The reference is now the Equaliser section of design/Attic Expressive.figcraft (1.2.0 and later), which the app matches.

Attic gets its own ten-band equaliser, and a song or an album can keep a setting of its own. This sheet draws it in the Expressive style: the entry in Now Playing's menu, the sheet in its states, the indicator on Now Playing, and the Settings page that edits the default.

The sheet always edits the setting the song is playing with. Which one that is, is the first thing in the sheet, and the actions that change it are the last.

What is new

  • Scope cell. A group cell that names what the bands change: “For all songs”, “For this album”, “For this song only”. The default is a round tonal glyph; a song or album of its own is squared off and filled with primary, the kit's sign for on. The same three glyphs (library_music, album, music_note) carry the scope everywhere.
  • Band graph. One drawing: ten bars that are the sliders, on a shared 0 dB line, with the engine's response as a soft shape behind them. The bars sit on a ten-octave axis, so each bump of the response is above its bar. Off, the bars lose their colour and the response is flat.
  • Preset chips in the editor cell, the kit's filter chips in one scrolling row: “Custom” and “Save as preset” first when the bands match no preset, then saved presets, then the eleven built in.
  • Actions under the editor. “Save for this song” (filled) and “Save for this album” (tonal) as a button group; “Use default” as an outlined button with what it removes beside it.
  • Indicator. A pill in Now Playing's header while the song plays with its own or its album's setting.

What was kept

  • The track menu, with “Sleep timer” where it was. “Equaliser” shares its group and its two-line form.
  • The sheet, group, chip, switch, dialog, menu and settings rows are the kit's own. The sheet over the player takes the cover's scheme, as the track menu does.
  • “Opens the equaliser on this phone.” keeps its wording and moves to the last row of the new page, under the title “Phone equaliser”.

Open questions

  • Edits made before “Save for this song” stay in the default. The sheet edits what is playing, so someone who drags a band and then saves for the song has changed every song first. Saving could instead put the default back to what it was when the sheet opened.
  • “Use default” cannot be undone. The app's snackbar has no action today. An “Undo” there would make the button safe to press.
  • Preset curves in these frames are placeholders; the engine owns the real ones.
