The offline music player you need.

No account, no streaming, no ads. Attic plays your own music files offline, and its source code is open under the GPL.

For Android 13 or newer on a 64-bit ARM phone. The APK is 58 MB, from GitHub Releases.

Two styles, one app

Expressive and Comfort each restyle every screen, in light and dark. In the app, switch in Settings > Appearance: the change applies at once and the song keeps playing. Pick a style here and every screenshot on this page changes with it.

Style

Colour taken from the album art and the phone's wallpaper, a wavy progress line, and buttons that change shape as you press them.

Soft rounded cards, a warm amber accent, and a dock at the bottom for Home, Search and Library. Album pages still take a tint from the cover.

What Attic does

Plays any folder, and tidies messy tags without touching the files

Copy music onto the phone, into Music, Download, an SD card or any other folder Android indexes, and Attic builds artists and albums from it. Artist/Album/song.mp3 becomes an artist and an album; a folder like Bollywood/ or Workout/ becomes a playlist.

Downloaded files often have no album tag and an uploader's name where the artist should be. Attic works out what the tags meant and shows that instead. Nothing is written back to your files.

Radio built from how your songs sound

Start radio from any song and Attic keeps ten similar songs in the queue. It finds them by analysing each song's sound on the phone, in the background while it charges. Nothing is uploaded.

  • Learns from skips, finishes and likes, in a listening log kept on the phone
  • Stays in the language you're listening to, with about one song in ten from another language when the match is strong
  • Spots the same song in another language and plays one version, so a Tamil session gets Aaruyire and a Hindi session gets Tere Bina, not both
  • Nine instant mixes on Home, from your favourites and unplayed songs that sound like them, changing daily

New in 1.1

Daily mixes from the songs you play

Home has three to five mixes. Each one groups songs you have played or liked that sound alike, and tops them up with songs you rarely play that sound like them. They are rebuilt once a day, at midnight.

  • On repeat: the songs you played most in the last 30 days
  • Haven't heard in a while: favourites and old regulars with no play in the last 60 days
  • Newly added: files that reached the phone in the last 30 days
Smart shuffle Playlists and Favourites
Shuffles the collection and plays a similar song from the rest of your library after every second one. The playlist itself never changes.
Genres and genre radio In Search
Browse by genre, taken from each file's genre tag or, for untagged songs, from how the song sounds. Any genre can start a radio.

Mixes, and genres for untagged songs, come from song analysis, which runs while the phone charges. After an update from 1.0, they fill in as analysis reaches each song.

Synced lyrics that work offline

The current line is highlighted, and tapping a line jumps to it.

  • Reads lyrics already on the phone, with no network: a .lrc file with the song's name in the same folder, or lyrics in the file's tags
  • Downloads the rest from LRCLIB, for one song or the whole library, and keeps them for offline use

Recap of your listening

Your top songs, artists and listening clock for the week, month, year or all time, counted from the log on your phone. Save or share any of the cards as an image.

Outside the app

  • Notification and lock-screen controls, including shuffle and like
  • Bluetooth and headset media buttons, and a pause when another app takes audio focus
  • A resizable home-screen widget with the current song, controls and five recent albums

Android Auto

  • Home's instant mixes, Playlists, Albums and Artists on the car's screen, with covers
  • Search, or ask by voice: "play Paper Lanterns on Attic"
  • Not yet tested in a car. If you try it, say how it went

Touch and sleep timer

  • Swipe a song row towards the end of the line to add it to the queue
  • Light haptics on like, play and pause, reordering and the queue swipe. They follow the phone's touch-feedback setting
  • A sleep timer pauses the music after 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes, or at the end of the song

Backup and first run

  • Back up favourites, playlists, listening history, saved lyrics and settings to a file, and restore it on any phone. A restore adds to what is there and deletes nothing
  • Android's own backup keeps the same data, encrypted end to end, and a new phone picks it up on first launch
  • On first run, pick a style and theme, allow access to your music, and Attic shows the songs it found

New in 1.1

Level volume, and a crossfade when you want one

Settings has a Playback section with three rows.

Volume levelling On by default
Plays songs at a similar loudness, from their ReplayGain tags or, without tags, from the loudness Attic measures on the phone. An album played in order shares one level, so its quiet and loud songs keep their balance.
Crossfade Off, or 2 to 12 seconds
Fades each song into the next. Songs from one album played in order still run straight into each other.
Equaliser The phone's own
Opens the equaliser already on your phone, applied to Attic's sound. The row shows only when the phone has one.

Loudness is measured during song analysis, which runs while the phone charges. A song that hasn't been measured plays as it is, so after an update from 1.0, levelling reaches more of the library as analysis goes on.

What leaves your phone

Attic has no account, no analytics, no ads and no crash reporting, and the developer receives no data from the app. Playback and song analysis never touch the network. The one request Attic makes itself is a lyrics lookup.

Questions

Is there an iPhone version?

No. Attic is for Android only.

Does it stream music?

No. Attic plays audio files stored on your phone or its SD card. There is no streaming service and no account.

Which formats does it play?

MP3, M4A, FLAC, Ogg, Opus and WAV.

Where does it look for music?

In any folder Android indexes, including Music, Download and an SD card. It leaves out ringtones, alarms, notification sounds, recordings, podcasts, audiobooks and chat voice notes.

Is there crossfade?

Yes. Settings > Playback > Crossfade blends songs over 2 to 12 seconds, and is off until you choose a length. Songs from one album played in order still run straight into each other.

Is there an equaliser?

Attic opens your phone's equaliser. Settings > Playback > Equaliser hands Attic's audio to the phone's sound settings or an equaliser app you have installed. The row only appears when the phone has one.

Does it work with Android Auto?

Attic supports Android Auto. In the car you can browse Home, Playlists, Albums and Artists, search, and say "play … on Attic". It hasn't been tried in a car yet, so reports from drivers are welcome.

Why do some songs have no genre, or play louder than others?

Songs without a genre tag get one from song analysis, and songs without ReplayGain tags get their loudness measured by it. Analysis runs while the phone charges, so after you add music or update Attic, genres and volume levelling fill in as it reaches each song, and until then a song plays at its own volume. Settings shows how far analysis has got, and "Analyse now without a charger" runs it straight away.

Which Android version does it need?

Android 13 or newer, on a 64-bit ARM phone. The release build has been tested on one phone so far, so reports from other phones are welcome.

Why is the APK 58 MB?

It carries the song analysis model and its runtime, which radio, mixes and genres use to compare how songs sound. That runtime is also why Attic only runs on 64-bit ARM phones.
