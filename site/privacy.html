Attic privacy policy

Effective 23 November 2026. Applies to Attic for Android, package app.attic.player, version 1.0 and later.

Attic is a music player for the audio files already on your phone. It has no account, no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting. The developer receives no data from the app.

What Attic reads on your phone

  • Your music. With the audio permission you grant, Attic reads the songs Android's media library lists, in any folder on the phone or its SD card: their tags (title, artist, album, duration), embedded cover art, any lyrics in the tags, and the audio itself. It leaves out ringtones, notification and alarm sounds, recordings, podcasts, audiobooks and voice notes.
  • Lyrics files beside your songs. With the same permission, Attic reads .lrc lyrics files that have a song's name and sit in its folder. Android lists these with your music.
  • A language label file, if you add one. Attic reads language-labels.tsv from its own folder under Android/data/app.attic.player/files/ if you put one there.

What Attic stores on your phone

All of this stays in the app's private storage on the device:

  • favourites and playlists
  • a listening log of plays, skips, finishes and likes, used for radio, instant mixes and Recap
  • the saved play queue and your settings
  • lyrics you have downloaded, and lyrics read from your music and .lrc files
  • the results of song analysis, which turns each song's sound into a list of numbers so radio can find similar songs, and any language you set for a song

Song analysis runs on the phone. No audio, and no analysis result, leaves the device.

Uninstalling Attic, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes all of it from the phone. Your music files are never changed or deleted, and neither are Recap images you saved to Pictures. Copies you made with the backups below are not deleted.

Backups

Android's backup. If backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android copies two things to your Google account, usually once a day:

  • a backup file Attic keeps in its private storage, with your favourites, playlists, listening log, downloaded lyrics, the languages you set and the saved queue. Each song is named by its file's path on the phone, such as Music/Hindi/Kabira.mp3.
  • your settings: theme, style and autoplay

Attic tells Android to send this only when the phone can encrypt it end to end, which needs Android 9 or later and a screen lock. Neither Google nor the developer can read it. Song analysis results are not sent; a phone restored from the backup analyses its songs again. When Attic is installed on a new phone signed in to the same account, Android restores the copy and Attic adds it to that phone's data. The developer receives nothing. Turning off backup in Android's settings stops it, and the copy already in your Google account is handled by Android and your account, not by Attic.

When you move to a new phone by cable or a direct phone-to-phone transfer, Android copies Attic's data, song analysis included, straight from the old phone to the new one, without going through a server.

Backup files you make. Settings > Backup > "Back up to a file" saves the same data, as a JSON file, to a place you choose in Android's file picker. If you choose a cloud folder, that service stores it under its own terms. Attic doesn't upload the file anywhere itself. The file is not encrypted, so keep it somewhere private: it contains your listening history and the names of your song files. "Restore from a file" reads a file you choose and adds it to what is on the phone.

What leaves your phone: lyrics lookups

Attic connects to the internet for one thing: downloading lyrics from LRCLIB, a free lyrics service with no account. It does this only when you tap to download lyrics for a song, or start a download for the whole library in Settings. Songs whose files already have lyrics are never looked up.

For each song, Attic sends an HTTPS request to lrclib.net containing:

  • the song's title
  • the first artist listed for the song
  • the album name, if the song has one
  • the song's length in whole seconds

If that finds no match, Attic sends a second request with a search query made of the title and the first artist. Each request also carries the header User-Agent: Attic/<version> (https://github.com/srivathsanvenkateswaran/attic), which gives the app's version number and the address of its source code.

No file, cover art, account, device identifier or listening history is sent. As with any web request, LRCLIB sees your IP address. LRCLIB is run independently of Attic and its own terms apply to what it does with requests.

Sharing and saving Recap cards

When you tap Share on a Recap card, Attic renders the card as an image and hands it to the app you pick in Android's share sheet. When you tap Save, it writes the image to Pictures/Attic on your phone. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you choose to share it.

Permissions

Permission Used for
READ_MEDIA_AUDIO Reading your music
POST_NOTIFICATIONS Playback controls, and progress while songs are analysed
FOREGROUND_SERVICE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK, WAKE_LOCK Playing with the screen off
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_DATA_SYNC Keeping song analysis running with its progress notification
INTERNET Lyrics downloads only
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED Added by WorkManager, the Android library that schedules song analysis, so scheduled work survives a restart

What Attic does not do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting. Attic includes no analytics or crash reporting library. ONNX Runtime, the library that runs song analysis, can report usage to Microsoft; Attic turns that off, so it sends nothing.
  • No ads and no advertising identifier.
  • No location, contacts, camera, microphone or other personal data.
  • No selling or sharing of data with anyone, because the developer collects none.

Children

Attic is not directed at children under 13 and collects no personal information from anyone.
